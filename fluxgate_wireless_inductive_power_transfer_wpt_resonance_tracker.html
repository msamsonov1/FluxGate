Electrodynamic Formulation & ZVS Resonance Tracking

Coupled Mutual Inductance Dynamics under Perturbed Lunar Permeability

In terrestrial resonant wireless chargers, secondary coil position is fixed or mechanically constrained. In lunar exploration, an autonomous rover approaches a stationary charging apron across loose regolith dust ($\text{FeTiO}_3$), craters, and boulder mounds. Changes in displacement vector $\mathbf{r} = (\Delta x, \Delta y, \Delta z)$ and spatial pitch $\theta$ cause extreme shifts in coupling coefficient $\kappa(\mathbf{r})$ and primary-to-secondary mutual inductance $M(\mathbf{r})$, detuning the system away from Zero-Voltage Switching (ZVS) and producing lethal thermal dissipation inside GaN bridge FETs.

1. Coupled Resonant Induction & Flux Linkage

Neumann Induction

Mutual inductance $M$ between primary pad coil $C_1$ and rover receiver $C_2$ is governed by the Neumann integral over closed contours with lunar spatial misalignment $\mathbf{R}_{12}$:

$$M(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 \mu_r}{4\pi} \oint_{C_1} \oint_{C_2} \frac{d\mathbf{s}_1 \cdot d\mathbf{s}_2}{\|\mathbf{r}_1 - \mathbf{r}_2\|}$$

The primary linked magnetic flux $\Phi_1(t)$ includes self and cross-coupled components modulated by the operating switching frequency $\omega = 2\pi f$:

$$\Phi_1(\omega) = L_1 I_1(\omega) + M(\mathbf{r}) I_2(\omega) e^{j\phi(\omega)}$$
Note: In lunar regolith, $\mu_r \approx 1.002$ to $1.04$ depending on ferromagnetic sub-millimeter iron ($\text{Fe}^0$) nanophase concentrations.

2. Zero-Voltage Switching (ZVS) Tank Inversion

Tank Impedance

To achieve soft-switching, the input impedance $Z_{\text{in}}$ seen by the primary GaN full-bridge must remain slightly inductive, satisfying the zero-voltage turn-on criterion:

$$Z_{\text{in}}(\omega, \mathbf{r}) = j\omega L_1 + \frac{1}{j\omega C_1} + \frac{\omega^2 M(\mathbf{r})^2}{R_L + j\omega L_2 + \frac{1}{j\omega C_2}}$$

The exact resonance pole $\omega_0(\mathbf{r})$ splits and shifts as mutual inductance $M(\mathbf{r})$ decreases during mechanical yaw, pitch, or roll:

$$\omega_{\text{res}}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{1}{\sqrt{L_1 C_1 \left(1 - \frac{M(\mathbf{r})^2}{L_1 L_2}\right)}} = \frac{\omega_0}{\sqrt{1 - \kappa(\mathbf{r})^2}}$$
Hard-switching penalty: If phase angle $\angle Z_{\text{in}} < 0$, capacitive discharge blows out GaN bridge within $<140\,\mu\text{s}$.

3. Sub-Surface Regolith Eddy Heating Dynamics

Poynting Loss

High iron lunar mare soils contain ilmenite ($\text{FeTiO}_3$) grains. The fringing magnetic field $\mathbf{H}(\mathbf{r}, t)$ generates parasitic loss $P_{\text{regolith}}$ through volumetric eddy dissipation:

$$P_{\text{eddy}} = \iiint_V \frac{1}{2} \sigma_{\text{regolith}} \|\mathbf{E}_{\text{ind}}\|^2 dV = \frac{\pi^2 \sigma f^2 d^2}{6 \rho} \hat{B}_{\text{fringe}}^2$$

FluxGate's predictive DSP minimizes fringing $\mathbf{B}_{\text{fringe}}$ by dynamically collapsing drive current when $\kappa(\mathbf{r}) < 0.12$:

$$\nabla \times \mathbf{E} = -\frac{\partial \mathbf{B}}{\partial t}, \quad \mathbf{J}_{\text{eddy}} = \sigma_{\text{eff}} (\mathbf{E} + \mathbf{v} \times \mathbf{B})$$
Excess regolith heating damages lunar cryo-instruments and degrades subsurface ice stability in permanently shadowed regions (PSRs).

4. Real-Time Spatial Inversion & Wheel Vector

Extended Kalman

By sampling primary bridge current $I_1$, sense voltage $V_1$, and resonant zero-crossings at 200 kHz, the DSP state observer inverts the spatial displacement gradient $\nabla_{\mathbf{r}} \Phi$:

$$\mathbf{v}_{\text{rover}} = -\mathbf{K}_p \left[ \nabla_{\mathbf{r}} \Phi(f, \mathbf{r}) \right]^{-1} \cdot (\Phi_{\text{target}} - \Phi_{\text{measured}})$$

The closed-loop steering command vector $\mathbf{u}_{\text{actuator}} = [v_x, v_y, \omega_z]^T$ drives the rover wheel hubs directly into the ZVS energy saddle point:

$$\mathbf{u}_{\text{actuator}} = \arg\max_{\mathbf{r}} \left( \eta_{\text{ZVS}}(f, \mathbf{r}) - \lambda P_{\text{loss}}(\mathbf{r}) \right)$$
